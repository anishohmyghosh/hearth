# The Hearth

An interactive, fire-lit map of healed masculinity: **wildfire into hearth**. Same fire, held well, so it warms instead of burning.

**Live:** https://anishohmyghosh.github.io/hearth/

## What's inside

- **The map.** Eight transformations around a central hearth: Suppression → Feeling, Isolation → Connection, Shame → Self-respect, Aggression → Protective strength, Control → Steadiness, Proving → Purpose, Armour → Embodiment, Fear of tenderness → Wholeness. Each opens a live wound-to-healed animation (auto-plays, or drag the slider), a placard with research, one practice and one affirmation, and numbered sources.
- **Meditation.** About 7 minutes of guided breathing at ~6 breaths per minute, with one suggestion per breath: settling, a body scan, the eight affirmations, a future-self visualisation and a gentle return. Optional generated drone and fire-crackle audio.
- **Tend the Fire (game).** Words fall as embers; steer the brazier to catch the ones that feed a healthy fire and let the smothering ones fall. A prompt at the top rotates through the eight themes, and matching words count double both ways. Reach +10 to blaze into the next level, hit −10 and the fire goes out. Combos, a slow-motion Breathe power (hold Space), rare golden hearthstones, wind from level 4, cold rain from level 5, and from level 3 the embers stop hinting, so you have to read. The game-over screen pairs every wound word you caught with its healed opposite. Ranks run from Spark to Keeper of the Hearth; enter a name for the top-10 board (saved in each player's browser). A total play count comes from [Abacus](https://abacus.jasoncameron.dev), a free counter service; if it's unreachable, the game works without it.
- **Sources.** 14 references, from the APA Guidelines for Psychological Practice with Boys and Men to research on emotion suppression, affect labelling, social connection, self-compassion and slow breathing.

A single self-contained `index.html`, no build step, no dependencies beyond Google Fonts.

> A reflective practice tool, not therapy. If something heavy comes up, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
